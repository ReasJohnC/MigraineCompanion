# Pineal Gland (Pineal Organ)

**Hook:** A tiny night-clock sits deep behind the thalamus, where sleep timing meets a special angle in the model.

## Where it is

The pineal gland is a small midline structure in the epithalamus, attached by a short stalk to the posterior roof of the third ventricle. In a mid-sagittal view, place it behind the posterior thalamus, below the splenium of the corpus callosum, and just above the midbrain’s superior colliculi.

## What it does

Established physiology: pineal cells produce melatonin, especially in darkness, as part of the body’s light-dark timing system. Melatonin helps signal biological night and supports circadian sleep-wake rhythm; it is not a simple sleep switch.

## The prodrome connection

Established migraine science: prodrome can include sleep disturbance, difficulty sleeping, fatigue, yawning, or changes in alertness before migraine onset. Because the pineal gland helps time melatonin rhythms, it is a reasonable educational marker for wakefulness or circadian disruption, though not proof that the pineal gland initiates migraine.

The theory: in the “Prodrome Zone” model, the pineal receives a distinct “special” force angle, separate from the thalamus and hypothalamus. The model proposes that when this angle strikes the pineal, melatonin/circadian signaling is disrupted, producing pre-migraine wakefulness.

## Caption

The pineal gland marks melatonin timing: established circadian biology, plus the special-angle migraine theory.
