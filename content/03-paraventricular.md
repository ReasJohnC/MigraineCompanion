# Paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus

**Hook:** A tiny hypothalamic hub where the brain helps tune thirst, urine, and body-fluid balance.

## Where it is

The paraventricular nucleus, or PVN, is a paired cluster in the medial hypothalamus, just to either side of the third ventricle. In a mid-sagittal brain view, place the marker slightly off the cut surface, beside the upper anterior wall of the third ventricle, below the thalamus and above the pituitary stalk region.

## What it does

Established physiology: the PVN contains neuroendocrine neurons involved in autonomic and hormone regulation. Some PVN neurons produce vasopressin, also called antidiuretic hormone, which helps the kidneys conserve water and helps regulate blood volume and osmotic balance.

## The prodrome connection

Established science: changes in thirst and urination can reflect shifts in fluid regulation, and vasopressin is one important hormone in that system. The PVN is not the only structure involved; thirst, kidney signaling, blood volume, and other hypothalamic and brainstem pathways all contribute.
