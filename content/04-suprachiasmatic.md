# Suprachiasmatic nucleus

**Hook:** The brain’s tiny timekeeper, sitting just above the crossing of the optic nerves.

## Where it is

The suprachiasmatic nucleus, or SCN, is a paired cluster in the anterior hypothalamus. In a mid-sagittal model, place it low and forward in the hypothalamus, directly above the optic chiasm and beside the lower wall of the third ventricle. It sits below the thalamus and well in front of the pineal gland.

## What it does

The SCN is the body’s master circadian clock. It receives light-dark information from the eyes and helps coordinate daily rhythms in sleep, alertness, hormone timing, temperature, and other body cycles. Through hypothalamic and pineal pathways, it helps shape melatonin timing and the brain’s sense of day and night.

## The prodrome connection

**Established science:** fatigue, difficulty sleeping, and changes in alertness can occur during the migraine prodrome, sometimes hours or days before migraine onset. The hypothalamus is a reasonable region to highlight for sleep-wake and body-rhythm symptoms, but current science does not prove that one prodrome symptom maps cleanly to the SCN alone.

**The theory:** in this model, an angled “energy force” passing through the Prodrome Zone may strike or disturb the SCN area, producing wakefulness, insomnia, listlessness, or circadian disruption before the attack.

## Caption

The SCN keeps circadian time; in this theory, its disturbance may explain sleep and wakefulness changes before migraine.
