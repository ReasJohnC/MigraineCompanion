# Lateral tuberal nucleus, “Mystery Spot”

**Hook:** A tiny hypothalamic marker for the uneasy “something is coming” feeling before migraine.

## Where it is

The lateral tuberal nucleus sits in the tuberal region of the hypothalamus, within the lateral portions of the tuber cinereum. In a mid-sagittal brain view, place its marker just off the exact midline, above the pituitary stalk/median eminence, behind the optic chiasm, and in front of the mammillary bodies. Because it is lateral, the marker should feel slightly tucked to the side of the third-ventricle floor rather than centered on it.

## What it does

Established anatomy places this nucleus inside the hypothalamus, a deep-brain control hub for body state: sleep-wake rhythm, hunger, temperature, hormones, autonomic tone, and stress-linked signaling. The specific function of the lateral tuberal nucleus in humans is not as clearly defined as nearby hypothalamic areas. It is best presented as a small hypothalamic landmark, not as a proven “mood switch.”

## The prodrome connection

Established migraine science recognizes mood change, irritability, fatigue, cravings, thirst, yawning, and other whole-body shifts as possible prodrome symptoms. The hypothalamus is often discussed in migraine research because it helps regulate internal body state and communicates with limbic circuits involved in emotion.

The theory: this nucleus is nicknamed the “Mystery Spot” because the model proposes that one angle of the migraine-initiating energy force passes through this part of the Prodrome Zone. In this model, that angle helps explain why some attacks begin not with pain, but with a broad, hard-to-name change in mood, patience, and inner balance.
