# The Different Angles of Force

**Hook:** One hidden crossing, many possible warnings.

## Where it is

This capstone is a map, not a single brain part. Place the fan’s center in the deep diencephalon around the third ventricle: thalamus above, hypothalamus below and forward, optic chiasm under the anterior hypothalamus, and pineal body tucked posteriorly. The paraventricular and suprachiasmatic nuclei sit near the midline hypothalamus; the lateral tuberal nuclei are lateral targets, so show them as paired off-midline points.

## What it does

Established: these regions participate in timing, arousal, homeostasis, endocrine/autonomic signaling, and sensory relay. The SCN helps coordinate circadian rhythms; the PVN contributes to neuroendocrine and autonomic regulation; the pineal releases melatonin in relation to light-dark timing. The thalamus, including posterior visual-attention regions such as the pulvinar, helps route sensory information.

## The prodrome connection

Established: migraine prodrome can include sleep changes, fatigue, mood shifts, hunger/thirst, frequent urination, and later, for some people, visual aura. The theory: an “energy force” enters the Prodrome Zone at different angles; one angle emphasizes lateral tuberal appetite/arousal pathways, another PVN-related fluid or stress signaling, another SCN wake-sleep timing, and a special posterior angle reaches the pineal. In this model, these angle-dependent strikes create different symptom profiles; a thalamus/pulvinar path is used to explain visual aura.

## Caption

The theory: different force angles select different deep-brain targets, shaping different warning signs before migraine.
